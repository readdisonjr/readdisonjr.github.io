Backseat Pain: Where Your Words Can't Steer

Literary family novel · November 7, 2026

Isaiah Wallace never had to ask where they were going. His older brother Darius drove—hands on the wheel, grinning in the mirror like the road was something he’d already won. Next summer, Darius promised, he’d teach him everything.

Then Isaiah’s phone lit up with Darius’s name, and he turned it face down.

Now the house is full of people bringing plates nobody eats. Their mother still sets six places. Their father disappears into the garage. Isaiah holds his sister and his little brother and folds his own hurt so small it fits behind his teeth. At night he sits in Darius’s car—backseat, always the backseat—talking to a driver who isn’t there.

Grief, he learns, is speaking from the backseat of a car with no one at the wheel. Every word just hits the glass.

Backseat Pain is a novel about the brother who was supposed to teach you how to drive, the call you didn’t answer, and what it takes to finally move into the front seat—not to leave him behind, but to carry him forward.
